We see that the the base model has the highest AUC. We then calculate the confusion matrix and see that we have low precision but high recall. This is because our model is incorrectly flagging many companies as bankrupt when they do in fact not go bankrupt. However, the high recall score does mean that the model is correctly flagging companies that are bankrupt as correct, which is the more important application. Thus, with a score of 0.9 our base model can correctly distinguish between bankrupt and non bankrupt companies very well.

It is important to note that even with a high AUC score it does not necessarily mean our model is necessarily excellent. This is because of the low precision score. Therefore, if one were to improve this whilst maintaining a high AUC, then it would lend credence into the belief that the model is truthfully “excellent”.
